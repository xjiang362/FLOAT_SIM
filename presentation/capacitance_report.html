Resolved along the way

Two long-standing puzzles closed themselves once the geometry was in hand. The two central-electrode datasets differed by a factor of 3.567, previously attributed to a voltage discrepancy — it is the area ratio of electrodes A and B (3.5420), so A is the outer, B is the inner, and the drive voltage was identical throughout.

Separately, the nominal gap is 15.454 µm, not the assumed 15.000 µm. That 3% error alone was capping the geometric capacitance model at R2 = 0.90; correcting it takes the same model to 1.0000.

1The one integral behind everything

A conducting plate sits above a fixed electrode at height rz, tilted by small angles θx and θy. The local gap over a point on the electrode is linear in position:

Local gap g(x,y) = G − y θx + x θy   with   G = gz − a5 rz

Capacitance accumulates as ε dA / g. This is the key structural difference from the force problem: electrostatic pressure goes as 1/g2 and yields rational integrals, whereas capacitance goes as 1/g and yields logarithmic ones.

Every footprint in this device — rectangle, hollow square, right triangle — is a region swept in y between two straight edges that are linear in y. Writing those edges as xL(y) = pL + qLy and xR(y) = pR + qRy, the double integral closes in terms of a single primitive:

Master result h(u) = u (ln u − 1)   // u is a LOCAL GAP, in metres

AE = G + pEθy     BE = qEθy − θx

C = (ε / θy) [ BR−1h(AR+BRy) − BL−1h(AL+BLy) ] ya yb

The x-integral contributes ln u; integrating that again in y gives h. Rectangles are the case qL = qR = 0; right triangles have one sloped edge with q = ±1. Hollow shapes are signed sums.

It is worth being explicit about what u is, since it is the only symbol here that carries physical meaning rather than bookkeeping. It is the local gap along an edge: substituting the edge xE(y) = pE + qEy into the gap expression gives

What u means u = AE + BEy = G − y θx + (pE + qEy)θy = g (xE(y), y)

So h is only ever evaluated at a physical gap, and u > 0 is precisely the condition that the plate does not touch the electrode — which is why the logarithm never has a negative argument for any valid pose.

Degenerate limits

Two limits need care, because the closed form is a small difference of large terms there. As θy → 0 the gap stops depending on x and the x-integral becomes algebraic; as B → 0 the prefactor and the bracket diverge together while the product stays finite. Both are handled by explicit series rather than by letting the formula cancel:

Series branch, B → 0 ∫ ln(A+By) dy ≈ (yb−ya) ln A + B (yb2−ya2) / 2A − B2(yb3−ya3) / 6A2
Verified

Checked against 2-D quadrature across every branch — generic tilt, θy→0, θx→0, both→0, tight gaps, rectangles and triangles. Worst relative error 1.7×10−11.

Worth noting how the first version of that check failed: integral2 takes its first argument as the outer variable, so the region was handed over transposed. At zero tilt the integrand is symmetric in x and y, so those rows passed while every tilted row failed — a pattern that pointed at the test, not the formula.

Why the exact form is needed

The perturbation parameter is y θx / g ≈ 0.22 at full tilt. Tilt is not a small correction here, and a linearised effective-gap model cannot represent it faithfully — at maximum tilt the exact capacitance is 13.2% above the flat-plate value.

3The floating proof mass

This is the part that makes the sensing problem interesting. Terminals 1 and 2 are coplanar triangles — they barely see each other directly. Their coupling runs through the proof mass, which is deliberately left floating.

Because the actuation electrodes are driven as differential pairs, the proof mass sits at a near-zero potential but is not tied to any node. Eliminating a floating node from a capacitance network is a star-to-mesh transform. The total capacitance presented at that node — the resonator capacitance — governs how much signal survives the trip:

Resonator node total SR = ∑ i CiR + Cpar   // 20 electrodes + suspension/shield parasitic

The sum runs over every conductor facing the plate: 8 actuation squares, 8 sensing triangles, 2 central-A halves, 2 central-B halves. All twenty come from the closed form. Only Cpar — the suspension and shield — cannot be had from a footprint integral.

Star-to-mesh elimination, then the two-port CM = C12 + C1RC2R / SR
C1 = S1 − C12 − C1R(C1R+C2R) / SR
C2 = S2 − C12 − C2R(C1R+C2R) / SR

Ceff = CM + C1C2 / (C1+C2)

The final term is easy to drop and expensive to omit: the measured two-port capacitance includes the series combination of each terminal's remaining path to ground, not just the through-path.

A diagnostic worth keeping

An earlier version left that series term out and let a fringing factor α float. The fit returned α = 10.9 at R2 = 0.99 — an excellent fit to a physically impossible parameter, since a fringing enhancement cannot exceed ~1.3. The independent zero-parameter check on C1R flatly contradicted it. A fitted parameter straying far outside its physical range is a structural error in the model, not a calibration.

4Calibration against COMSOL

Terminal identities were established by correlating each Maxwell-matrix entry against the analytic curves, so the assignment is an identification rather than an assumption.

Terminal 21 is the proof mass — its off-diagonal sum is 5.3×10−11 F, twenty times any electrode. Terminals 1 and 2 are the quadrant-1 sensing triangles (correlation 0.9975), 3/4/7/8/11/12/15/16 the actuation squares, 17/18 the central-A halves and 19/20 the central-B halves.

The real finding is gz. The assumed 15.000 µm was wrong by 3%, and that single number was what held the geometric model at 0.90.

5The same method on the force model

Capacitance integrates 1/g; electrostatic pressure integrates 1/g2. The same decomposition applies, and it sharpens the existing quadrant actuation model considerably.

For an offset rectangle spanning y ∈ [y1, y2], integrating the pressure gives the product of the near-edge and far-edge gaps — not one lumped effective gap squared:

Offset electrode, exact Fz = k V2 ∕ [ (g − y1θ)(g − y2θ) ]

τ = k V2 f(s1,s2) ∕ (θ g)   // si = yiθ∕g, DIMENSIONLESS
f = [(1−s1)(1−s2)]−1 + ln [(1−s2)∕(1−s1)] ∕ (s2−s1)

si is deliberately a different letter from the u of section 1. Both describe the gap at an electrode edge, but u is that gap in metres while si is the fraction of the gap that tilt consumes, so that 1 − si = (g − yiθ) / g is the normalised edge gap. si → 1 is contact; the 0.22 quoted earlier as the perturbation parameter is exactly this quantity at full tilt.

The torque expression contains no new parameters: k, y1 and y2 are all pinned down by the force fit. Torque is therefore a prediction, not a fit — so agreement is validation.

The small-angle limit of f is (s1+s2) / 2, giving τ → Fz (y1+y2) / 2 — the lever arm is the electrode centroid. That is what the old four-coefficient moment-arm polynomial had been approximating, and it explains what its leading coefficient physically was.

6Status and what is still open

Settled

  • The closed form is exact — verified to 1.7×10−11 against quadrature on every branch.
  • Geometry independently reproduces three previously-fitted quantities to 0.70%, 0.10% and 10−14.
  • Central electrode A is the outer, B the inner; their 3.567× difference is area, not voltage.
  • The nominal gap is 15.454 µm.
  • Quadrant torque is predicted with zero free parameters at R2 > 0.998.

Open

  • Ceff sits at 0.96 held-out, against 1.0000 for its dominant input. The loss is in assembly, not in the geometry — most likely the shield-coupling terms S1, S2, currently single constants. Shield geometry would let them be derived too.
  • In-plane degrees of freedom are untested. rx, ry and θz are identically zero throughout the dedicated sweep, so their residual correlations are undefined. They were previously argued negligible; that argument has not actually been tested against data.
  • Corner rounding. The derivation assumes straight edges. Any chamfer or fillet in the real layout would appear as a small systematic residual.
  • Central electrode B's torque still lacks a clean fit; its sweep varied rz, which dominates Fz and leaves the tilt term as a poorly-conditioned residual.
Recommendation

The highest-value next input is the shield and suspension geometry. Cpar is 43% of the resonator node and is currently the single largest fitted quantity in the model; deriving it the way the electrodes were derived would close most of the remaining gap in Ceff.
